Trained models

Two models were trained on a driver drowsiness dataset and are combined into the final drowsy / not-drowsy verdict: a CNN (YOLOv8n-cls, fine-tuned) that looks at the face image, and a gradient-boosting classifier over the landmark measurements and 52 facial expression scores. The rule-based state below explains why (eyes closed, yawning, nodding), and the live monitor's timers still use it.

The signals
Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR)

Six landmarks around each eye give EAR = (|p2−p6| + |p3−p5|) / (2·|p1−p4|). An open eye is around 0.25–0.35; it falls towards 0 as the lids close. It's fused with MediaPipe's eyeBlink blendshape score for robustness.

Mouth Aspect Ratio (MAR)

Average inner-lip opening divided by mouth width. A wide, sustained opening means a yawn. It's fused with the jawOpen blendshape.

Head pitch

Derived from the 3-D facial transformation matrix. A large forward tilt (chin dropping to chest) indicates nodding off.

Live monitor logic
  • Calibration: during the first ~3 s the driver's normal open-eye EAR is measured and the closed-eye threshold is set to 75% of it.
  • Drowsy alarm: eyes closed continuously for ≥ 1.5 s, or head nodding for ≥ 2 s. A beep sounds and a snapshot is saved to history.
  • PERCLOS: percentage of time the eyes were closed over the last 60 s. Above 15% raises a fatigue warning.
  • Yawn counter: yawns lasting ≥ 1 s are counted. 3 or more in 5 minutes raises a fatigue warning.
  • Face lost: no face for ≥ 3 s shows a "driver not visible" warning.
